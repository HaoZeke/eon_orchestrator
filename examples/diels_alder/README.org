* Overview

The Diels-Alder reaction is a classic [4+2] cycloaddition between a conjugated
diene (butadiene) and a dienophile (ethylene) to form a cyclohexene ring. This
example demonstrates why IRA alignment is *critical* for multi-molecular reactions.

* Source Structures

Reactant and product structures are from:

#+begin_quote
R. Goswami, M. Gunde, and H. Jónsson, "Enhanced climbing image nudged elastic

arXiv:2601.12630. doi: [[https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2601.12630][10.48550/arXiv.2601.12630]].
#+end_quote

The parent Diels-Alder reaction (butadiene + ethylene → cyclohexene) serves as
a benchmark for multi-molecular NEB calculations.

* Why This Demonstrates IRA Value

Unlike simple isomerizations (HCN) or single-molecule substitutions (SN2), the
Diels-Alder reaction involves:

1. *Two separate molecules* approaching from ~4 Å separation
2. *Simultaneous bond formation* at two positions (concerted mechanism)
3. *Ring closure* with significant structural reorganization
4. *Complex atom mapping* (16 atoms, non-obvious correspondence)

Without IRA alignment:
- Linear interpolation would place molecules on collision courses
- Initial IDPP path would have extreme atomic overlaps
- NEB optimization would likely fail or converge to wrong saddle

With IRA alignment:
- Correct relative orientation identified via Hungarian algorithm
- Translation and rotation removed before interpolation
- IDPP path shows realistic approach trajectory

** Transition State Geometry

The TS should show:
- C₂ᵥ symmetry (synchronous bond formation)
- Two forming C-C bonds (~2.2 Å, vs 1.54 Å in product)
- Planar arrangement of reacting carbons
- Imaginary frequency corresponding to symmetric ring closure

* Comparison with Simpler Examples

| Feature | HCN | SN2 | Diels-Alder |
|---------+-----+-----+-------------|
| Atoms | 3 | 6 | 16 |
| Molecules | 1 | 2 (ion + neutral) | 2 (neutral) |
| IRA value | Minor | Moderate | *Critical* |
| Barrier | ~1.8 eV | ~1.1 eV | ~1.2 eV |
| Complexity | Linear | Backside attack | Ring closure |

The Diels-Alder reaction is the most realistic test of IRA alignment because:
- Multiple independent molecules must be correctly oriented
- No obvious "reaction coordinate" like in SN2
- Ring formation requires coordinated motion of many atoms
